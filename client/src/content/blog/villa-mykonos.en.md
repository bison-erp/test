If you plan to buy a villa in Mykonos, you are joining buyers from across Europe, the US and the Middle East drawn by Cycladic light, a very strong tourist season and easy access from Athens. Yet the island is also one of the most tightly regulated markets in Greece, with a freeze on permits outside the town plan, tougher enforcement against illegal construction and specific golden visa rules. This guide covers the areas, prices, the process for foreign buyers and rental potential.

## The best areas to buy a villa in Mykonos

Each coast has its own character. The right choice depends on how you will use the home (summer family base, seasonal letting, entertaining), its exposure to the strong northerly wind known as the meltemi, and its distance from Chora, the main town.

### Psarou and Agios Lazaros

On the south coast, a few minutes from Chora, Psarou and Agios Lazaros (above Platis Gialos) are the most exclusive addresses: beaches sheltered from the meltemi, well-known beach clubs and hillside villas with sea views. Supply is very limited and prices sit at the top of the range.

### Aleomandra and Agios Ioannis

On the west coast, Aleomandra and Agios Ioannis enjoy the most sought-after sunsets, looking towards Delos and the nearby islets. Close to Chora and Ornos, they suit a summer residence as well as a villa intended for high-end letting.

### Houlakia

To the north-west, near Chora, Houlakia is quieter and known for its pebble beach and sunsets. It is more exposed to the meltemi, which can limit time on the terraces on some summer days, so it is worth visiting on a windy afternoon before deciding.

### Elia and Kalo Livadi

On the south-east coast, Elia and Kalo Livadi offer long beaches, larger plots and a more unspoilt setting. Properties here tend to be more spacious, around twenty minutes from Chora, and appeal to families looking for privacy.

Value is driven above all by the view, shelter from the meltemi, distance to a beach, build quality and, crucially on Mykonos, the **planning compliance** of the property.

## Building permits and illegal construction: the key risk

Mykonos is under close scrutiny from the Greek state. After a series of illegal building cases, the Ministry of Environment suspended new building permits outside the approved town plan, pending a Special Urban Plan for the island. A draft put out for consultation in November 2025 would extend the freeze to the end of 2026. Inspections backed by satellite imagery have identified unauthorised pools, outbuildings and basements, and penalties can go as far as demolition.

What this means for a buyer:

- favour a completed villa whose every element (pool, annexes, underground levels) matches the permit;
- be wary of a plot sold "with a permit": an unused permit may be caught by the current freeze;
- insist on the engineer's certificate confirming there are no unauthorised structures, or that they have been legalised, which is required for the transfer;
- have any past legalisation reviewed, as the government has said it will not open loopholes for illegal building on the island.

## How foreign buyers purchase property in Greece

A typical purchase follows these steps:

1. **Greek tax number (AFM)**: required to sign. It is issued by the tax office, usually through your lawyer. Opening a Greek bank account is advisable.
2. **Greek lawyer**: checks the chain of title at the land registry and the Hellenic Cadastre (Ktimatologio), mortgages, planning status and easements. A notarised, apostilled power of attorney means you do not need to travel for each stage.
3. **Border-area permission**: some regions of Greece, including certain Aegean islands, are classified as border areas. Non-EU buyers then need prior approval, which can take from several weeks to several months. Your lawyer will confirm whether the property is affected.
4. **Engineer**: compliance certificate and technical survey.
5. **Notary**: drafts and executes the deed of sale, which is then registered with the cadastre.

On costs, sources agree on the orders of magnitude: **3.09% transfer tax** on resale properties (new builds may fall under VAT, under a regime to be checked), notary fees of about 0.8–1% plus VAT, registration of around 0.5%, and legal fees of roughly 1–2% plus VAT. Ask for a detailed budget before making an offer.

## Mykonos villas and the golden visa

Mykonos is one of the areas where the [Greece golden visa](/golden-visa-greece "Greece golden visa: thresholds, process and support") threshold is **€800,000**, along with Attica, Thessaloniki, Santorini and islands with more than 3,100 inhabitants. Elsewhere in Greece the threshold is €400,000, and €250,000 for converting commercial premises into housing or restoring listed buildings. As a general rule, the investment must be a single property of at least 120 m². Our article on the [Greece golden visa 2026 thresholds](/blog/greece-golden-visa-2026-thresholds "Greece golden visa 2026: thresholds and zones") covers the rules in detail.

One key point: since Law 5100/2024, a property bought to obtain or renew a golden visa **cannot be let on a short-term basis** (Airbnb-style). The stated penalty is a €50,000 fine and revocation of the residence permit. Long-term letting remains possible. Have a Greek lawyer confirm your own situation before committing.

## Rental potential of a Mykonos villa

Outside the golden visa route, a villa on Mykonos can generate significant seasonal income: rates published by rental agencies run from about €10,000 to more than €100,000 per week in high season, depending on size and location. However, the season is short (mainly June to September), management, concierge and maintenance costs are high, and short-term letting requires registration with the Greek tax authority. No return is guaranteed, and each project needs its own numbers.

:::faq
### How much does a villa in Mykonos cost?
Based on 2026 listings, villas start at around €1.5–2 million in secondary areas and often exceed €8–10 million for exceptional estates in Psarou or Agios Lazaros. Prime villas generally trade between €8,000 and €15,000 per m².

### Can foreigners buy a villa in Mykonos?
Yes. EU citizens buy on the same terms as Greek nationals. Non-EU buyers may need prior approval if the property lies in a designated border area, which your lawyer checks at the outset.

### Does a Mykonos villa qualify for the Greek golden visa?
Yes, from an investment of €800,000, generally in a single property of at least 120 m². The property cannot then be let short-term. Have a Greek lawyer confirm your situation.

### Can I rent out my Mykonos villa on Airbnb?
Yes, if the property was not used for a golden visa, once registered with the Greek tax authority and in line with local rules. If it supports a golden visa application, short-term letting is prohibited.

### Can I build a new villa on Mykonos?
New permits outside the town plan are suspended, and the freeze was set to be extended to the end of 2026. Buying an existing, fully compliant villa is currently the safest route.
