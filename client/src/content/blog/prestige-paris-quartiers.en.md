Choosing between luxury real estate Paris neighbourhoods is less about the arrondissement average than about the street, the floor, the view and how rare the property is. This guide covers the seven areas most sought after by international buyers, sets out price ranges per square metre for prime homes in 2026 and explains how the market has moved since 2025.

## What counts as luxury real estate in Paris?

There is no legal or official definition of a "prime" or "prestige" property in France. Professionals look at a combination of factors:

- **Location**: a quiet street in a central arrondissement, close to a garden, the river or a landmark.
- **Building**: Haussmann-era cut stone, a private *hôtel particulier* or a 17th or 18th-century building, with a concierge and well-kept common areas.
- **The home itself**: the *étage noble* (traditionally the second floor), ceiling height, parquet, mouldings, fireplaces, dual or triple aspect, a terrace, an open view and parking.
- **Scarcity**: more than 200 m², through apartments, private gardens, no overlooking neighbours.

On price, one benchmark is often quoted. According to Green-Acres' Bonne Fortune observatory, it takes around **€1.9 million** to enter the top 10% of the Paris market (against about €1.7 million in the Alpes-Maritimes and Var). In practice, most firms treat the Paris luxury segment as starting **from roughly €2 million**, which represents only a small percentage of sales in the city.

## The most sought-after neighbourhoods for luxury property in Paris

### 6th arrondissement: Saint-Germain-des-Prés and the Luxembourg Gardens

The 6th is the most expensive arrondissement on average and, according to the notaries, one of the strongest performers over the past year. The most coveted addresses surround the Luxembourg Gardens, Rue de Fleurus, Place Saint-Sulpice, Rue Jacob and the Quai de Conti and Quai Malaquais. Stock consists of older buildings, often without an original lift, and large family apartments are scarce: a home of more than 150 m² overlooking the gardens tends to sell quickly, frequently without ever being advertised.

### 7th arrondissement: Faubourg Saint-Germain, Invalides, Champ-de-Mars

The 7th is home to ministries, embassies and some of the finest private mansions in the city. The Faubourg Saint-Germain (Rue de Varenne, Rue de Grenelle, Rue de l'Université) appeals to buyers who want calm and volume. Around the Champ-de-Mars and Avenue de Suffren, a view of the Eiffel Tower commands a clear premium, and homes with a private garden or courtyard sit at the top of the range.

### 8th arrondissement: the Golden Triangle

Bounded by Avenue Montaigne, Avenue George-V and the Champs-Élysées, the Golden Triangle is the territory of couture houses, palace hotels and headquarters. Residential stock is thinner than elsewhere, which supports values. Rue François-Ier, Rue Jean-Goujon and Rue Marbeuf are the most desirable streets to live on. Further north, Parc Monceau offers a more residential setting, with large Haussmann apartments and a handful of private mansions.

### 16th arrondissement: Passy, Trocadéro, La Muette

The 16th remains the family choice: generous floor areas, well-run buildings, highly regarded schools and the Bois de Boulogne on the doorstep. Trocadéro and Avenue Henri-Martin offer Eiffel Tower views; Passy and La Muette have a village feel; Avenue Foch and the gated Villa Montmorency sit at the very top. For the same size, the price per m² is often lower than on the Left Bank, which makes the 16th a common trade-off for larger families.

### 4th arrondissement: the Marais and Île Saint-Louis

The Marais attracts international buyers with its 17th-century mansions, Place des Vosges and lively streets, and homes with exposed beams, a cobbled courtyard and complete quiet are the ones that move first. Île Saint-Louis is a micro-market of its own: sales are so rare that each one is an event, especially for river-view apartments on the Quai d'Orléans, Quai de Béthune or Quai d'Anjou.

### 1st arrondissement: Palais-Royal, Tuileries, Place Vendôme

The 1st has a small stock of high-end homes: apartments overlooking the Palais-Royal garden, facing the Tuileries from Rue de Rivoli, or close to Place Vendôme and Rue Saint-Honoré. These addresses suit a Paris base used a few months a year, with services and luxury shopping within walking distance. Homes with a direct garden view are extremely rare.

### Neuilly-sur-Seine: the residential alternative

Just outside the 16th and 17th, Neuilly-sur-Seine offers family apartments, houses and mansions with gardens, something almost impossible to find inside the city limits. Boulevard Maurice-Barrès facing the Bois, the Saint-James area and Île de la Jatte are the most sought after. The average price per m² is below the central arrondissements, but a house with a garden can reach figures comparable to the finest Left Bank apartments.

## Paris prime market trends for 2025 and 2026

After falling by around 12% from the 2022 peak, Paris prices stabilised and edged up again at the end of 2025 (roughly +1.8% according to the Paris Chamber of Notaries). The top end held up better than the wider market, supported by buyers who rely less on mortgage finance.

In 2026 the market remains selective. Barnes reported a fall of about 10% in sales volume in Paris over the first five months of the year compared with 2025, alongside a concentration of deals on the rarest homes and a return of international buyers, notably from the United States. In practice:

- flawless properties (floor, view, condition, outdoor space) sell quickly and close to the asking price;
- homes needing work, or poorly placed within the building, leave real room to negotiate;
- many of the best opportunities never reach the portals, which is why [off-market property in Paris](/off-market-paris "Off-market property in Paris: how we source it") deserves a dedicated approach.

### What budget do you need for a luxury property in Paris?
There is no official threshold. According to Green-Acres' Bonne Fortune observatory, around €1.9 million is needed to enter the top 10% of the Paris market. Most professionals place the luxury segment from roughly €2 million, while also looking at the quality and rarity of the home.

### Which is the most expensive arrondissement in Paris in 2026?
The 6th tops the published averages, at roughly €14,400 to €16,400 per m² depending on the source and method. For exceptional homes, the 6th and 7th compete for the highest prices, with the best addresses trading above €25,000 to €30,000 per m².

### Will prime Paris property prices fall?
Nobody can promise either way. After the 2022–2024 correction, prices have stabilised and rare properties remain in high demand. Barnes reported lower volumes in early 2026, which gives buyers more room to negotiate on less exceptional homes than on flawless ones.

### Is Neuilly-sur-Seine a good alternative to central Paris?
For a family looking for a house with a garden or a large, quiet apartment, yes. The average price per m² is lower than in the central arrondissements, but houses and mansions reach high figures because they are so scarce.

### Can foreign buyers purchase property in Paris freely?
Yes. France places no restrictions on property purchases by non-residents. You will still need to budget for notary costs, open a bank account, document the source of funds and take tax advice suited to your country of residence.
