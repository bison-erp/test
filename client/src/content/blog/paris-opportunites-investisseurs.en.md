There is an enduring investment principle often quoted by experienced market participants: *"Buy when the cannons roar and sell when the trumpets sound."*

The expression captures a fundamental truth about financial and real estate markets: some of the most compelling acquisition opportunities emerge precisely when confidence weakens, sellers become more cautious and buyers increasingly prefer to remain on the sidelines.

Against the backdrop of France's current political and social climate, this principle deserves renewed attention.

Political instability, episodes of civil unrest, economic uncertainty and approaching electoral developments are contributing to a climate of apprehension among certain French property owners. Yet, for international investors with available capital, disciplined acquisition strategies and a long-term wealth preservation perspective, this environment may offer an opportunity to acquire selected French real estate under more favourable conditions.

## A More Uncertain France, More Negotiable Sellers

Real estate markets are never entirely insulated from their political and economic environment.

Fiscal policy, institutional stability, borrowing costs, consumer confidence and expectations regarding future taxation all influence residential property transactions.

As France approaches future electoral milestones, some domestic property owners are increasingly concerned about the potential implications of political change for their wealth. Questions surrounding taxation, fiscal policy and economic stability may encourage certain owners to accelerate disposals or reconsider their property holdings.

Others, confronted with longer marketing periods and more selective buyers, may be increasingly prepared to negotiate.

This does not mean that the French residential market is experiencing a uniform collapse in property values. Rather, it reflects a more selective market in which asking prices and achieved transaction prices can diverge significantly.

For international buyers, this distinction is crucial.

The opportunity is no longer simply to identify a desirable property and purchase it at the advertised price. It is to identify motivated sellers, assets with genuine intrinsic value and transactions in which negotiation can materially improve the economics of an acquisition.

In today's environment, acquisition discipline may prove considerably more valuable than market timing alone.

## French New-Build Residential Property: Developers Under Increasing Commercial Pressure

France's new-build residential sector is experiencing particularly significant difficulties.

Reduced reservation volumes, tighter financing conditions, the withdrawal of certain tax incentives and elevated construction costs have placed considerable pressure on property developers.

In order to maintain sales momentum, preserve cash flow and reduce unsold inventory, developers are increasingly relying on commercial incentives and negotiated acquisition terms.

Depending on the programme and the developer's financial circumstances, buyers may encounter incentives such as:

- Notary fees covered by the developer,

- Selective commercial discounts,

- Upgraded specifications and additional finishes,

- Kitchen or furnishing packages,

- Tailored payment arrangements,

- Negotiated incentives on selected units.

For investors with substantial liquidity, these arrangements may create attractive acquisition opportunities, particularly in well-located residential developments with strong long-term rental or resale potential.

However, the new-build market requires a different approach from the resale market.

Developers generally have limited flexibility to reduce headline prices indiscriminately, as doing so may affect the financial structure of their developments. Commercial incentives and individual negotiations may therefore be more effective than publicly advertised price reductions.

Sophisticated buyers should consequently focus on the **total acquisition cost**, rather than simply the advertised price per square metre.

Developer credibility, delivery schedules, contractual protections, construction quality, location and future resale liquidity remain essential components of a sound investment decision.

In a market under pressure, negotiated acquisition terms can be as important as the purchase price itself.

## Paris: A Prime Residential Market Unlike Any Other

Political uncertainty may weaken confidence in certain French markets. It does not, however, eliminate the structural appeal of Paris.

The French capital occupies an exceptional position in the international residential property landscape.

Its architectural heritage, cultural influence, global connectivity, educational institutions, diplomatic presence and distinctive lifestyle continue to attract international families, entrepreneurs, investors and private wealth.

Paris also benefits from an increasingly important characteristic in prime residential investment: **structural scarcity**.

Exceptional Haussmannian apartments, historic townhouses, private mansions, properties with terraces, unobstructed views and residences in prestigious districts represent a limited and largely irreplaceable supply.

A distinguished apartment in Saint-Germain-des-Prés, a prestigious residence in the 7th arrondissement, an exceptional property in the Golden Triangle or a remarkable address in the 16th arrondissement is more than a residential asset.

It represents an international address, a tangible store of wealth and, in many cases, a legacy asset intended to be held across generations.

This is precisely why Paris continues to attract international capital even when the French economic and political environment becomes less favourable.

Prime residential real estate in Paris is not immune to market cycles. Nevertheless, exceptional properties may demonstrate greater resilience than more conventional residential assets, particularly when their location, architectural character and scarcity are genuinely distinctive.

## International Investors: Deploying Capital While Others Remain Cautious

International property investors frequently approach Paris from a different perspective than domestic homeowners.

For some, an acquisition represents geographical diversification of their wealth. For others, it is a European pied-à-terre, a family residence, a strategic residential asset or an investment intended for intergenerational wealth transfer.

Their investment criteria often extend beyond short-term capital appreciation.

Location, architectural integrity, long-term patrimonial value, ownership security, rental potential and future resale liquidity may carry greater weight than immediate market sentiment.

When domestic buyers hesitate, internationally mobile investors with secured financing or available liquidity may benefit from a more favourable negotiating environment.

This is particularly relevant in the prime and ultra-prime residential segments, where acquisition opportunities are frequently determined by individual circumstances rather than broad market movements.

The objective is not to purchase Paris indiscriminately.

It is to identify properties that possess characteristics that cannot easily be replicated: exceptional locations, architectural distinction, scarcity and enduring international appeal.

## Buy When the Cannons Roar: An Acquisition Philosophy, Not a Political Bet

The expression *"Buy when the cannons roar and sell when the trumpets sound"* is particularly relevant to long-term real estate investment.

Periods of uncertainty can create temporary discrepancies between collective perceptions of risk and the intrinsic value of selected assets.

When apprehension dominates the market, some sellers prioritise liquidity. When confidence eventually returns, competition among buyers may intensify and negotiating margins can narrow.

International investors may therefore benefit from acting when acquisition conditions are favourable, rather than waiting for the return of universal optimism.

This approach, however, requires rigorous due diligence.

Political and economic risks, taxation, ownership costs, rental regulations, financing conditions and exit liquidity must all be carefully assessed.

The objective is not to speculate on political instability, but to acquire quality assets at prices and under conditions that justify a long-term investment.

In prime real estate, discipline, selectivity and access to the right opportunities frequently matter more than attempting to predict the precise turning point of a market cycle.
