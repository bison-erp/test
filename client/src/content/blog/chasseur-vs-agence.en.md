Buyer's agent vs estate agent in Paris is one of the first choices an international buyer faces when looking for an exceptional apartment. Both professionals hold the same French licence, know the same streets and sometimes view the same homes. Yet they do not work for the same person. Understanding that difference tells you who defends your interests when a price of several million euros is on the table.

## Buyer's agent vs estate agent in Paris: who represents whom?

### The estate agency works for the seller

A prime estate agency is instructed by the owner under a **sales mandate**. Its job is to sell the property at the best price and on the best terms for its client, the seller. It treats buyers professionally and must give them accurate information, but its duty of loyalty runs first to the seller. Its fee rises with the price achieved.

### The buyer's agent works for you

A buyer's agent, also called a property finder or buying agent, is instructed by you under a **search mandate**. They search, shortlist, analyse, negotiate and see the purchase through to completion. They have no stock of homes to move: their interest is that you buy the right property at the right price.

In France, both professions fall under the Hoguet Act of 2 January 1970: a transaction licence, a prior written mandate, a financial guarantee and professional indemnity insurance. Neither may receive a fee before the sale is actually completed.

### When one firm plays both roles

Some agencies also accept search mandates. This is lawful, but it can create a conflict of interest when the agency shows its buying client a home that it is also instructed to sell. Ask the question directly: "If you show me a property from your own book, whose side are you on in the negotiation?"

## Who pays what: how fees are structured

The two models are paid in different ways.

- **The estate agency**: its fee is set in the sales mandate. It is usually payable by the seller and built into the advertised price, or sometimes payable by the buyer, in which case the listing must show the net price and the fee separately. Published scales commonly range from about 4% to 8% including VAT, decreasing as prices rise; in Paris, a study by the iad network found an average of around 5.4% including VAT on a €450,000 apartment.
- **The buyer's agent**: their fee is paid by the buyer, on top of the price. Market practice in Paris generally ranges from 2% to 5% of the purchase price including VAT, or takes the form of a flat fee. ## Access to homes, including off-market listings

An agency first shows its own portfolio, then possibly homes from partner agencies that share instructions. To see the whole market, a buyer has to deal with many agencies at once.

A buyer's agent has no portfolio to protect. They contact every agency, notaries and wealth managers, and make direct approaches. That is a decisive advantage in the unlisted segment, which accounts for a significant share of high-end sales in Paris. To see how these homes circulate, read our guide to [off-market property in Paris](/blog/off-market-property-paris-guide "Off-market property in Paris: how to access homes that are never listed").

## Negotiation: who defends your price?

At an agency, the negotiator has to close a sale the seller finds acceptable. They pass on your offer and may support it, but they cannot advise you to lower it at their client's expense.

A buyer's agent prepares the negotiation on your behalf: comparable sales from the Paris notaries' data and the public DVF database, time on the market, technical defects, upcoming building works, the seller's motivation. They draft the offer, choose the timing and the conditions precedent, then coordinate with your notary. No outcome is guaranteed, but every argument is used in your interest.

For buyers based abroad, this also covers practical points that agencies rarely handle: explaining the French sequence of preliminary contract, cooling-off period and completion, checking that the signing timetable fits an international transfer of funds, and making sure the notary has everything needed for identity and source-of-funds checks well before the deadline. These details often decide whether an offer is accepted over a competing one.

## Which one is right for your purchase?

### An agency may be enough when…

- you live in Paris and have time to view;
- you know the market and prices by neighbourhood well;
- you have spotted a specific home listed by a known agency;
- the purchase is simple: main home, standard financing, no time pressure.

### A buyer's agent makes sense when…

- you live abroad or travel frequently;
- you are looking for a rare property, often sold without advertising;
- the purchase is complex: buying through a company, international financing, major renovation, mixed use;
- you want an independent view on the price before committing.

The two are not mutually exclusive. A buyer's agent works with prime agencies every day, bringing them a qualified, prepared and responsive buyer, which agencies welcome. ## How to choose your buyer's agent

If you decide to be represented, check a few simple points:

1. **The professional licence** and its number, the financial guarantee and indemnity insurance.
2. **The search mandate**: scope, duration, any exclusivity, fees and termination terms.
3. **Independence**: does the firm also hold sales mandates? How does it handle a potential conflict?
4. **Method**: number of preliminary viewings, content of the reports, support through to completion.
5. **International experience**: languages spoken, familiarity with non-resident buyers, coordination with your advisers.

:::faq
### Is a buyer's agent more expensive than an estate agency?
They are not the same fee. Agency fees are usually included in the price and paid by the seller, while a buyer's agent's fee is paid by the buyer on top of the price. The right comparison is the total cost of acquisition after negotiation.
### Can a buyer's agent help with a home already listed by an agency?
Yes. The buyer's agent can arrange the viewing, analyse the property and negotiate on your behalf with the agency holding the sales mandate. It is best to appoint them before your first viewing.
### Does a buyer's agent see the same homes as estate agencies?
They see listed homes from every agency, plus unlisted homes circulating through notaries, fellow professionals and private networks. They have no portfolio of their own to favour.
### Can a Paris luxury agency refuse a buyer who has an agent?
In practice this is rare: the agency wants to sell, and a represented buyer is usually well prepared. How the two professionals work together is agreed case by case.
### Do I have to sign an exclusive mandate?
No, it is not compulsory. An exclusive mandate for a limited period does, however, allow the agent to commit fully to your search. Check its duration and termination terms before signing.
:::
