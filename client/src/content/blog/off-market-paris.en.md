Off-market property in Paris means homes sold without any public advertising: no portal listing, no shop window, no social media campaign. For demanding buyers, this discreet circuit is often where the rarest apartments, private mansions and whole buildings change hands. The question is how these homes circulate, why their owners avoid publicity and how to reach them without taking unnecessary risks.

## What does off-market property in Paris actually mean?

A home is "off-market" when the owner is willing to sell but refuses any advertising. It is shown to a small circle of qualified buyers, usually through a professional or a notary, sometimes directly between private parties.

Two neighbouring situations are often confused with it:

- **The "pre-launch" listing**: the property will be published within weeks, and the agency first shows it to its best contacts.
- **The "not yet for sale" home**: the owner has decided nothing but could sell if a serious offer arrived. This is the territory of direct approaches, which take longer and are less certain.

No official statistic measures the share of off-market sales. Neither the Paris notaries' data (Notaires du Grand Paris) nor the public DVF transaction database records whether a sale was advertised. Specialist Paris firms estimate that roughly a third of prime sales close without publication, and more above €5 million. These are practitioners' estimates and should be read as orders of magnitude, not market data.

## Why Paris owners choose to sell discreetly

The reasons are rarely aesthetic. They almost always reflect the seller's personal situation:

- **Privacy**: public figures, business leaders and families who do not want their interiors photographed and published online.
- **Inheritance or separation**: heirs or former spouses want to sell quickly without exposing a disagreement.
- **Wealth restructuring**: a family office or private bank reorganises a portfolio and prefers a controlled disposal.
- **Price testing**: the owner sounds out the market at an ambitious price without "burning" the property publicly.
- **Security**: an apartment filled with art or collections is not meant to be visited by dozens of curious onlookers.
- **Relocation abroad**: moving country imposes a timetable and remote management.

For buyers, this means an off-market seller is not always in a hurry, nor always open to negotiation. Above all, they expect a credible, decisive and discreet counterpart.

## The channels through which unlisted homes circulate

### Notaries

In Paris, notarial offices handle many estates and gifts, and some of them also negotiate sales. The family notary is often the first to know that a property will be sold. They share that information only with people they know and can vouch for.

### Building managers and concierges

The managing agent and the building concierge know the life of a building: an elderly owner moving out, an estate being settled, major works ahead. Their role is limited to signalling an intention to sell. Under French law, anyone without a professional licence cannot negotiate a sale, and any introducer's fee is tightly regulated.

### Family offices and private banks

Wealth managers advise clients who sell as often as they buy. When a property has to be sold, they look for a solvent, discreet buyer, often before instructing any agency.

### Agencies and fellow professionals

Prime estate agencies hold confidential instructions that they never publish. A buyer represented by a recognised professional gains access more easily, because the agency knows the client is qualified and their funding has been checked.

### The personal network

Lawyers, tax advisers, architects, art dealers, entrepreneurs: part of the opportunities simply start with a conversation. This network takes years to build and cannot be bought.

## Risks and legal framework for unlisted sales in France

Discretion does not suspend the rules. It calls for more vigilance, not less.

### The Hoguet Act and written mandates

In France, the Hoguet Act of 2 January 1970 governs every form of property brokerage. The professional must hold a transaction licence and a **written mandate** before acting, recorded in a mandate register. On the seller's side, an off-market home is therefore covered by an ordinary sales mandate with a no-advertising clause. On the buyer's side, the buyer's agent works under a search mandate setting out the assignment and the fee.

Article 6 of the Act forbids the licensed professional from receiving any payment before the transaction is actually concluded. An intermediary asking for an "access fee" to a confidential list should raise a red flag.

### Risks specific to discreet sales

- **A price never tested by the market**: without open competition, the asking price may be disconnected from notarial references.
- **Incomplete information**: surveys, minutes of co-ownership meetings, building rules and easements must be gathered as for any sale. The energy performance certificate and mandatory surveys are still required at the preliminary contract.
- **Fake off-market**: a home presented as confidential may have been shown to many buyers already, which weakens your position.
- **Time pressure**: "you must decide tonight" is sometimes a sales line rather than a reality.
- **Compliance**: professionals are bound by anti-money-laundering rules. Prepare proof of identity and source of funds early: it is also what makes your offer credible.

## How a buyer's agent sources off-market homes

A buyer acting alone, especially from abroad, rarely sees these homes. A buyer's agent's job is to be recognised by the right people as the representative of a serious client. The following is a typical assignment, described for illustration only:

1. **The brief**: neighbourhoods, size, floor, outdoor space, outlook, total budget including costs, timetable and financing constraints.
2. **Qualification**: proof of funds or a bank agreement in principle, and the intended ownership structure (personal name, French property company, foreign entity).
3. **Activating the channels**: notaries, fellow agents, wealth managers and targeted direct approaches in specific buildings.
4. **Shortlisting**: preliminary viewings, price analysis against comparable sales, review of co-ownership documents.
5. **Negotiation**: drafting the offer, conditions precedent and signing timetable.
6. **Securing the purchase**: coordinating with your notary, your lawyer and, where needed, your bank until completion.

## Checklist before committing to an unlisted Paris home

- Does the person presenting the home hold a written mandate from the seller, or are they acting for you under a search mandate?
- Is the price consistent with recent sales in the building and the street?
- Have you received the last three sets of co-ownership meeting minutes, the building rules and the surveys?
- Has the floor area been certified under the Carrez law?
- Are major works voted or foreseeable (facade, roof, lift)?
- Does the proposed timetable fit your financing and tax planning?
- Who pays the fees, and are they clearly stated in the preliminary contract?
- Is your file (identity, source of funds, ownership structure) ready?

## Unlisted or listed: do you have to choose?

No. A listed home can be an excellent opportunity if it has stayed online too long, and an off-market home can be overpriced. The sound strategy covers both markets: monitor public listings while activating confidential channels. What matters is the quality of the analysis and the speed of decision when the right home appears.

:::faq
### Is an off-market home in Paris always cheaper?
No. Some sellers accept a discount in exchange for discretion and speed, while others test an ambitious price. Only a comparison with recent sales in the area can tell you whether the price is right.
### Can I buy an unlisted property without an agent?
Yes, a direct private sale is possible, usually through the seller's notary. In practice, access to these homes runs mostly through professional networks, which give priority to qualified, represented buyers.
### Are off-market sales legal in France?
Yes. A professional may sell without advertising as long as they hold a written mandate from the seller that complies with the Hoguet Act. The buyer's information rights, including mandatory surveys, are the same as in any other sale.
### How long does it take to find an off-market home in Paris?
There is no standard timeframe. It depends on how rare the property is, the budget and how flexible the brief is. A precise brief and financing in place shorten the search considerably.
### Should I pay to see a list of off-market properties?
No. In France, a licensed property professional cannot receive any payment before the sale is concluded. Be wary of any request for an upfront fee to view a confidential list.
:::
