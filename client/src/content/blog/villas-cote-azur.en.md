Off-market villas on the French Riviera are properties their owners do not want on the portals: waterfront estates, Belle Époque villas on the capes and family homes passed down through generations. This guide covers the main micro-markets, the price ranges seen in 2026, how the market moves through the year and the checks you need before buying.

## Why so many Riviera villas sell off-market

Along the coast between Saint-Tropez and Menton, a large share of exceptional properties changes hands without any public marketing. There are several reasons:

- **Seller discretion**: business owners, wealthy families and public figures do not want their address, their wealth or photographs of their interiors online.
- **Scarcity**: the capes are almost fully built and France's coastal protection law, the *loi Littoral*, strictly limits new construction. Every sale is an event, and owners often prefer to test a price quietly.
- **The buyer profile**: demand is international (continental Europe, the UK, the US, the Middle East) and flows through advisers, family offices and buyer's agents rather than through listings.
- **Protecting value**: a property that sits online for months loses its appeal. A confidential sale avoids that.

For a buyer, this means that a search limited to public listings shows only part of the market. A [French Riviera buyer's agent](/real-estate-french-riviera "French Riviera buyer's agent: prestige and off-market villas") works with local agencies, notaries, wealth managers and the owners themselves.

## Off-market villa micro-markets on the French Riviera

### Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat

The peninsula between Nice and Monaco is one of the most expensive residential addresses in Europe. Engel & Völkers' 2026 waterfront ranking recorded asking prices of up to around €113,000/m² for the most exceptional properties: a ceiling, not an average. Data published by PAP in summer 2026 puts the average for houses at around €39,000/m², based on very few sales. Sea-view villas generally start at several million euros, and waterfront estates reach several tens of millions.

### Cap d'Antibes

Between Juan-les-Pins and Antibes, the cape mixes large estates hidden among pine trees, contemporary villas and seafront properties. The west side, facing the sunset and the Lérins Islands, and the area around La Garoupe are the most sought after. Listings seen in 2026 for villas close to the sea or with sea views range from roughly €15,000 to €33,000/m² of living space, with waterfront homes and private sea access priced above that.

### Saint-Tropez and Ramatuelle

As Savills points out, Saint-Tropez is above all a family wealth market where people buy to enjoy and to pass on rather than for rental income. According to PAP (July 2026), the average house price in Saint-Tropez is close to €28,000/m², and around €21,000–22,000/m² in Ramatuelle, where values have eased after the sharp rise of 2020–2023. Les Parcs de Saint-Tropez, a private gated estate, along with Pampelonne and the Route des Plages, hold the most coveted villas, usually quoted as a total price rather than per m².

### Cannes: La Californie and Super-Cannes

On the hills above Cannes, La Californie and Super-Cannes offer villas with panoramic views over the bay and the islands, often larger than homes at the water's edge. Prices vary widely with view, plot and condition, and this is an area where homes needing work still leave room to negotiate.

## Seasonality: when to search for a Riviera villa

The prime villa market follows the seasons:

- **September to November**: after the summer, many owners decide to sell. This is often the best time to see off-market homes and to visit without tenants.
- **March to June**: buyers return and want to sign before the summer, so competition is stronger.
- **July and August**: homes are occupied or let, which makes viewings harder, but it is the right moment to judge noise, crowds and traffic.

From first viewing to completion, a purchase generally takes several months. We never promise a timeframe: it depends on the market and on the complexity of the file.

## Due diligence before buying a coastal villa in France

A Riviera villa often combines legal constraints that overseas buyers are unfamiliar with. Ask the notary, a planning lawyer and an architect to check:

- **The *loi Littoral***: the 100-metre strip from the shoreline is in principle unbuildable outside built-up areas, including for extensions. A plan to enlarge an existing villa can therefore be refused. The planning certificate (*certificat d'urbanisme*) and the local plan (PLU) set out the rules.
- **The coastal footpath easement**: properties bordering the public maritime domain can be subject to a 3-metre pedestrian right of way along the shore (Article L. 121-31 of the French Planning Code).
- **Permits and compliance**: confirm that the house, pool, outbuildings and extensions were authorised and formally completed. Some irregularities cannot be regularised.
- **Risk zones**: the risk statement (less than six months old) shows the applicable prevention plans, notably forest fire and coastal flooding, whose rules can restrict works.
- **Protected sites**: parts of the capes are listed or registered sites, or require the opinion of the heritage architect (*Architecte des Bâtiments de France*).
- **Technical points**: drainage, retaining walls, access and utilities.

## Buying a villa in France as a non-resident

France places no restrictions on property purchases by foreign nationals. A typical process looks like this:

1. **Offer and negotiation**, then a preliminary contract (*promesse* or *compromis de vente*) signed with the notary, with a deposit usually around 5–10% of the price.
2. **A 10-day cooling-off period** for a private buyer of a residential property.
3. **Conditions precedent** (financing, planning, pre-emption rights) and preparation of the deed, usually over two to three months.
4. **Completion** at the notary's office, in person or by power of attorney.

Budget around 7–8% in acquisition costs for an existing property (mostly transfer duties), proof of the source of funds, and tax advice on the holding structure, France's real estate wealth tax (IFI) and the council tax on second homes, which many coastal towns have increased.

:::faq
### What is an off-market villa on the French Riviera?
It is a villa offered for sale without any public listing. The owner presents it only to a small circle of agencies, buyer's agents and qualified buyers, to protect their privacy and the value of the property.

### How much does a villa in Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat cost?
Sea-view villas generally start at several million euros, and waterfront estates reach several tens of millions. Published averages for houses are around €39,000/m², with asking prices far higher for exceptional homes.

### Can you extend a seafront villa in France?
Not always. Within the 100-metre coastal strip, the *loi Littoral* in principle prohibits new buildings and extensions outside built-up areas. The local plan, risk prevention plans and any protected-site status add their own rules, so have your project checked before signing.

### Can foreigners buy a villa in France?
Yes, with no restriction based on nationality. The purchase goes through a French notary, the buyer documents the source of funds and can sign by power of attorney. Tax advice is recommended to choose the right holding structure.

### When is the best time to buy on the Riviera?
Autumn, from September to November, is often favourable: owners decide to sell after the season and properties are free for viewings. Spring tends to be more competitive.
