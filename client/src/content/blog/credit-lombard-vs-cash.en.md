## What is a Lombard credit?

A Lombard credit is a bank loan secured by the pledging (nantissement) of financial assets: a securities account, life insurance policy, PEA (share savings plan), fund units, bonds, or euro-denominated funds. The borrower retains ownership of the portfolio, which continues to generate returns (dividends, coupons, unrealized capital gains), but pledges it as collateral to the bank. The bank lends a percentage of the retained value of the assets (typically between 40% and 80% depending on their nature and liquidity). The loan is often non-purpose or can be specifically allocated to real estate, and is frequently repaid on an interest-only (in fine) basis---interest during the term, capital at maturity---over short, renewable periods (1 to 5 years).

Unlike a conventional mortgage, the collateral is the financial portfolio rather than the property being acquired. This fundamentally changes the situation in terms of speed, regulatory constraints, and wealth strategy.

## Preserving invested capital and creating leverage

The primary advantage lies in preserving the assets. If you already hold a well-performing portfolio and have cash available, using that cash to buy the property means immobilizing capital that could continue to generate a return higher than the cost of the loan.

In 2026, Lombard credit rates generally range from around 2.9% to 4%, depending on the institution and market conditions (often indexed to Euribor or €STR plus a margin). If the pledged portfolio generates an expected net return higher than this cost (for example, 5% to 7% over a long horizon), the difference constitutes a positive spread. The buyer keeps their asset allocation, continues to benefit from compound interest and diversification, and still acquires the property. The cash remains available for other opportunities, a cash buffer, or new investments. This is the essence of smart leverage: using debt to finance one asset while letting another asset work and produce a higher return.

## Tax optimization: avoiding the flat tax and preserving tax wrappers

The second advantage, often decisive in France, is tax-related. Selling securities or making a partial redemption of a life insurance policy to raise cash immediately triggers taxation of unrealized capital gains. At the 2026 flat tax rate of 31.4% (12.8% income tax + 18.6% social levies), a €100,000 capital gain costs more than €30,000 in tax. Pledging the assets, by contrast, is not a disposal: no capital gains tax is triggered, the tax seniority of the wrappers (the eight-year life insurance clock, PEA seniority) is preserved, and the tax reference income does not increase.

Moreover, when the property is a rental investment taxed under the real regime, the interest on the Lombard loan is deductible from rental income (Article 31 of the French Tax Code), provided there is clear traceability of the funds to the notary or eligible expenses. For an investor taxed at 30% or higher, part of the cost of the credit is effectively borne by the State through the tax reduction. The cash itself remains free to be reinvested in a tax-efficient manner.

## Flexibility, speed, and operational simplicity

A Lombard credit can be set up in a few days to a few weeks, compared with several months for a conventional real estate loan. It generally does not require borrower insurance, does not need a notarial deed for the collateral, and is not subject to the same HCSF debt-to-income caps as a standard mortgage (although the interest may be taken into account in the effort rate calculation when combined with another loan). The use of the funds is often free, offering significant flexibility: full financing of the purchase, an enhanced down payment for a conventional loan, or a bridge operation.

For a buyer who wants to seize an opportunity quickly ([off-market property](/off-market-paris "Off-market property in Paris"), forced sale, inheritance), this speed is a genuine competitive advantage. At the same time, the cash remains available for ancillary costs, renovations, another acquisition, or simply as a safety cushion.

## Concrete comparison and precautions

Imagine a property priced at €500,000. Paying cash immediately immobilizes €500,000. With a Lombard loan secured against a portfolio of at least €700,000 to €1,000,000 (depending on the advance rates), you finance the purchase while keeping the portfolio invested and the cash free. Over five years, if the portfolio returns 6% net and the Lombard loan costs 3.5%, the annual difference represents several tens of thousands of euros, before even considering potential tax deductibility and real estate capital gains.

These advantages should not obscure the risks. The rate is often variable, exposing you to rising costs. A margin call can occur if the value of the pledged assets falls significantly: the bank may then demand additional collateral or partial repayment. The interest-only structure requires a clear exit strategy (sale of the property, timely reallocation of investments, or refinancing). Finally, for a pure primary residence, the lack of interest deductibility reduces the tax advantage.

## Conclusion

When you have 100% of the purchase price of a property available in cash, a Lombard credit is not a default solution, but a powerful wealth-optimization tool. It allows you to preserve the return potential of your investments, avoid immediate taxation on capital gains, maintain your liquidity, and---in the case of a rental investment---benefit from the deductibility of interest. In an environment where financial markets offer attractive returns and French taxation weighs heavily on disposals, borrowing against your financial assets rather than using your cash can significantly improve the overall performance of your wealth. As always in wealth management, the arrangement must be calibrated with an adviser, taking into account your risk profile, holding horizon, and repayment strategy.
