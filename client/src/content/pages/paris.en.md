## Why use a buyer's agent in Paris?

The top end of the Paris market is small and opaque. The finest apartments (a *piano nobile* with a full-length balcony, a top floor with a terrace, an open view over the Seine or a monument) are rare, and a share of them changes hands without ever being advertised. For a buyer based in London, Dubai, Geneva, Hong Kong or New York, following that market from abroad is close to impossible.

A buyer's agent in Paris brings three things:

- **Wider access**: the public listings, but also the properties offered quietly through notaries, wealth managers, family offices, partner agencies and owners themselves.
- **A filter**: we view first, discard what does not fit and show you only the properties worth a trip.
- **An advocate**: negotiation from your side of the table, and a critical reading of the technical and legal documents before you commit to anything.

We handle family apartments, pieds-à-terre, private mansions, whole buildings and prime offices for companies and investors.

## Buyer's agent or luxury estate agency: what is the difference?

It comes down to the mandate. A luxury estate agency in Paris usually holds a **sale mandate**: it is instructed and paid by the seller to achieve the highest price. A buyer's agent holds a **search mandate** signed with the buyer: the job is to secure the right property at the right price.

| | Luxury estate agency | Buyer's agent (Acropolis) |
|---|---|---|
| Client | The seller | The buyer |
| Properties offered | Its own listings | The whole market, listed or not |
| Negotiation | Aims for the highest price | Aims for a fair price for the buyer |
| Due diligence | File supplied by the seller | Critical review on the buyer's behalf |
| Fees | Paid out of the sale price | Paid by the buyer, on success only |

The two roles are complementary: we work with the established Paris agencies and review their stock alongside the rest of the market. ## Our six-step process

This is how a typical engagement runs. Every project is different, and we adapt the pace to yours.

1. **Briefing**: intended use (main home, pied-à-terre, rental investment, offices), total budget, areas, must-haves and timing.
2. **Search mandate**: a written mandate sets out the scope and the fees, as required by the Hoguet law, which governs property intermediaries in France.
3. **Search and shortlist**: monitoring of listed stock, off-market sourcing through our network, first viewings by us, reports with photos, floor plans and a price analysis.
4. **Accompanied viewings**: grouped over a short visit, or by video call if you are abroad.
5. **Negotiation and offer**: a price case built on comparable sales, appropriate conditions precedent, and a drafted offer.
6. **Through to completion**: review of surveys and building documents, coordination with your notary, lender and advisers, and attendance at signing.

## Due diligence: what we check before you commit

In Paris, a beautiful apartment can sit in a poorly maintained building or a divided co-ownership. Before any firm offer, we review in particular:

- **Mandatory surveys**: energy performance certificate (DPE), asbestos, lead, electrics, gas, termites and the certified floor area. The energy rating matters to investors: since 2025, G-rated homes can no longer be offered on new tenancies.
- **The building's co-ownership records**: minutes of the last three general meetings, building regulations, maintenance log, approved or planned works, the multi-year works plan, service charges and any arrears.
- **Planning and permitted use**: compliance of past works, merged units, change of use for offices or short-term furnished lettings.
- **The notarial timeline**: preliminary contract, a ten-day cooling-off period for a private buyer of a home, then the final deed, usually two to three months later.

We work with your own notary or introduce you to Paris practices used to foreign buyers.
