Off-market property in Paris means homes and buildings sold without public marketing: no portal listing, no agency window, no photographs online. The owner chooses discretion and offers the property to a handful of serious buyers introduced by professionals they trust. Acropolis Real Estate acts only for buyers: we find these properties, check them thoroughly and negotiate on your behalf.

## The Paris off-market property market at a glance

There are no official statistics on unadvertised sales, because notaries record a transaction without knowing whether it was ever listed. Industry professionals generally estimate that 15% to 25% of transactions in the most sought-after arrondissements (6th, 7th, 8th and 16th) take place without a public listing, and that the share rises markedly above €2 million. Treat these as orders of magnitude, not hard data.

Market conditions matter too. According to the Notaires du Grand Paris, the average price of existing apartments in Paris held steady at around €9,600 per m² in the first quarter of 2026, while prime arrondissements such as the 8th and 16th were showing year-on-year falls at the end of 2025. In a calmer market, many sellers prefer to test a price quietly rather than leave a property online for months.

## Why owners sell without advertising

The reasons are often personal, and understanding them is part of negotiating well:

- **Privacy**: public figures, business owners and families who do not want their interiors or address exposed.
- **Estates and separations**: situations where a quick, quiet solution is preferred.
- **Price testing**: a seller sounds out a few buyers before deciding on a formal launch.
- **Buildings and offices**: an investor disposing of an asset without alerting tenants or competitors.
- **Security**: high-value homes, art collections and sensitive addresses do not suit open viewings.

## Off-market versus listed: what changes for the buyer

| | Listed property | Off-market property |
|---|---|---|
| Visibility | Every buyer in the market | A few pre-qualified buyers |
| Competition | Often strong for rare homes | Limited, but the seller chooses the buyer |
| Information | Listing, photos, price history | File shared on request, sometimes incomplete |
| Price | Public asking price, structured negotiation | More open, in either direction |
| Timing | Known schedule | Decisions are often quick |
| Due diligence | The same | The same, and all the more important |

An off-market home is not automatically cheaper. It is less exposed: the advantage lies in access and in negotiating without a bidding war. Our job is to check that the asking price is consistent with comparable sales, not to make you pay a premium for scarcity.

## The legal framework: the Hoguet law and due diligence

Off-market does not mean outside the rules. In France, the Hoguet law of 2 January 1970 governs all property intermediation: a professional must hold a licence and a written mandate before acting. On the seller's side, the property is covered by a sale mandate, often with a confidentiality clause. On the buyer's side, we act under a search mandate that defines the assignment and the fees.

Disclosure obligations are the same as for any sale. Before you commit, we check in particular:

- **Mandatory surveys**: energy performance certificate (DPE), asbestos, lead, electrics, gas, termites, natural-risk report and certified floor area.
- **Co-ownership records**: minutes of recent general meetings, building regulations, maintenance log, approved works, the multi-year works plan, service charges and arrears.
- **Title and planning**: the seller's title, easements, compliance of past works and the permitted use of the premises (residential or office).
- **Timeline**: preliminary contract before a notary, a ten-day cooling-off period for a private buyer of a home, then the final deed, usually two to three months later.

A discreet sale can move fast. That is exactly when these checks protect the buyer.

A property sold without advertising: no online listing and no agency window. It is shown only to a small number of buyers, through trusted professionals.

Not necessarily. The absence of public bidding can ease negotiation, but some sellers ask a premium for rarity. We always benchmark the price against recent sales.
