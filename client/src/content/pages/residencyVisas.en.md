**Residency by investment in Europe** allows a non-EU national to settle in, or simply hold a right of residence in, a European country in return for an investment. The rules have changed considerably in recent years: some countries have closed their schemes, others have raised thresholds or excluded real estate altogether. Here is an overview of the options in our markets, and of how we align the choice of property with the immigration process.

## Residency by investment in Europe in 2026

There is no single European scheme: each state sets its own conditions. For investors who want to go through property, the field has narrowed. Spain abolished its golden visa in April 2025, and Portugal removed real estate from its eligible investments in 2023. Among our markets, Greece is the country where buying property still leads directly to a residence permit.

One point first: citizens of the European Union, the European Economic Area and Switzerland need no visa to settle in another EU country. The options below concern third-country nationals.

## The options at a glance

| Country | Route | Minimum investment | Does property qualify? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greece | Investor residence permit (Golden Visa) | €250,000, €400,000 or €800,000 depending on area and property type | Yes |
| Luxembourg | Investor residence permit | €500,000 in a company, €3 million in a management structure or a €20 million bank deposit | No, real estate is excluded |
| France | Long-stay visitor visa | No investment amount: sufficient, regular resources | No, though owning a home supports the application |
| France | Talent passport (economic investor) | At least €300,000 invested in a business | No, a productive investment is required |

Sources are given country by country below. These rules change: always have your situation confirmed by a lawyer in the relevant country before committing.

## Greece: residence through property purchase

The Greek scheme grants a five-year residence permit, renewable for as long as the investment is held, to the investor and close family. Since 2024 the thresholds have been:

- **€800,000** in Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and on islands with more than 3,100 inhabitants;
- **€400,000** in the rest of the country;
- **€250,000** for converting commercial premises into housing or restoring listed buildings.

In the general case, the investment must be in a single property of at least 120 sq m. Properties bought under the scheme may not be used for short-term lets. There is no requirement to live in Greece to keep the permit, which also allows travel within the Schengen area within the limits that apply to residence permit holders.

## Luxembourg: an investor permit without property

Luxembourg offers a residence permit for third-country investors. According to the official Guichet.lu portal, there are four routes: €500,000 in an existing Luxembourg company (maintaining employment for five years), €500,000 in a new company (creating at least five jobs within three years), €3 million in a management and investment structure with genuine substance in Luxembourg, or a €20 million deposit with a Luxembourg bank held for at least five years.

The key point: **investments in buying or renting real estate are not taken into account**. Buying an apartment in Luxembourg City does not, on its own, confer a right of residence. ## France: the visitor visa and the talent passport

France has no property golden visa: owning a home grants no right of residence. Two routes are commonly used by affluent clients:

- **The long-stay visitor visa**: for people with sufficient resources who undertake not to work in France. Health insurance and accommodation must also be evidenced. The visa serves as a residence permit for one year and is then renewed as a visitor residence card. A Paris apartment or a Riviera villa then provides solid proof of accommodation.
- **The talent passport for economic investors**: according to prefecture guidance, it requires an investment of at least €300,000 in tangible or intangible assets of a company that the applicant runs or in which they hold at least 30% of the capital, with jobs created or safeguarded. Residential property does not qualify.

:::faq
### Which European country still offers residency through property investment?
Among our markets, Greece is the country where buying property leads directly to a residence permit, from €250,000, €400,000 or €800,000 depending on the area and property type. Luxembourg and France offer other routes, which do not accept real estate as a qualifying investment.

### Does buying property in France give me the right to live there?
No. Owning a home confers no right of residence in France. A third-country national can, however, apply for a long-stay visitor visa if they have sufficient resources; the property then serves as proof of accommodation.

### Can I obtain residence in Luxembourg by buying an apartment?
No. The official Guichet.lu portal states that real estate investments are not taken into account for the investor residence permit. The permit is obtained through investment in a company, a management structure or a bank deposit.

### Can my family benefit from the Greek Golden Visa?
Yes. The Greek permit generally covers the spouse, children under 21 and the parents of both spouses. The exact family members eligible should be confirmed by a Greek lawyer.

### Do I have to live in the country to keep the permit?
For the Greek Golden Visa, no minimum stay is required for renewal. In France and Luxembourg, by contrast, residence permits assume genuine residence in the country.
