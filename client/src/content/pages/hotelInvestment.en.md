## The hotel investment market in Greece

The demand fundamentals are strong. According to Bank of Greece data reported by Enterprise Greece (March 2026), the country welcomed around **38 million visitors in 2025** (+5.6%) and generated **€23.6 billion in travel receipts** (+9.4%), both records. Receipts are growing faster than arrivals, a sign of higher spending per visitor.

| Indicator | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor arrivals 2025 | about 38 million (+5.6%) | Bank of Greece, via Enterprise Greece |
| Travel receipts 2025 | about €23.6bn (+9.4%) | Bank of Greece, via Enterprise Greece |
| Hotel transaction volume 2025 | about €1.1bn, fifth-largest market in Europe | HVS, as reported in the trade press |
| New 4- and 5-star hotels | more than 450 in five years | Enterprise Greece |
| Preferred European destination for hotel investors | 43% of respondents | Hospitality Investor survey, June 2026 |

Two caveats matter. First, hotel performance lags the headline tourism figures: the Hotel Institute of Greece (ITEP) reported broadly flat summer occupancy in 2025. Second, short-term rentals compete directly with hotels on the islands. Differentiated, well-located and well-run assets outperform; the rest do not.

## Yields: ranges, never promises

There is no reliable public benchmark for Greek hotel yields comparable to those published for offices or retail. Some Greek market participants quote gross yields of roughly 6% to 10% a year for well-run boutique hotels. Treat these as indicative only: net yield, after operating costs, major maintenance and tax, is materially lower and varies widely between assets.

What actually drives returns:

- average daily rate (ADR) and revenue per available room (RevPAR): in Athens, RevPAR rose by about 6.8% in the first half of 2026 according to CoStar;
- the length of the season and the share of direct bookings;
- the cost base: staff, energy, platform commissions;
- the operating model: owner-operated, management contract, lease or franchise;
- the capital expenditure (capex) needed to stay at the level of the hotel's category.

We build a prudent business plan with you, with downside, base and upside scenarios. **No return is guaranteed.**

## Structuring the acquisition: Greek company options

Hotels are rarely bought in a personal name. There are two routes: buying the property and business (asset deal) or buying the operating company (share deal). A share deal also takes on the company's tax and employment history, hence the importance of due diligence.

| Vehicle | Minimum capital | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| IKE (private capital company) | no meaningful minimum | Smaller hotels, flexible structure, often single-shareholder |
| AE (société anonyme) | €25,000 | Larger projects, board of directors, bond financing possible |

The right choice depends on your personal tax position, any European holding company and the financing. It is decided with Greek corporate and tax advisers. We work with yours or introduce you to independent firms.

## Licences, classification and planning

Hotel operation is mainly governed by Law 4276/2014, under the Ministry of Tourism. What we always check:

- **the operating licence** (Special Operating Label, or ESL), issued within the framework of the Ministry of Tourism and the Greek National Tourism Organisation (EOT / GNTO), which must match the number of rooms and beds actually in use;
- **the star rating**, assigned through the Hellenic Chamber of Hotels, and whether it reflects the property's real condition;
- **planning compliance** of every building, pool and extension, checked by an engineer;
- **local constraints**: Mykonos and Santorini are under building-permit freezes until the end of 2026 at the latest, and the Special Spatial Framework for Tourism presented in May 2026 classifies these islands as saturated areas, with tighter limits on new hotels.

In practice, an extension plan on a high-demand island may prove impossible. We have this confirmed before any offer.

## Seasonality and operations

On the islands, the trading season generally runs from April or May to October; Athens and, to a lesser extent, Crete trade more evenly through the year. Enterprise Greece reports a sharp rise in December 2025 receipts, but the shoulder season is a growth opportunity rather than a certainty.

The operating model is a structural decision. A private investor who does not want day-to-day involvement will usually prefer a management contract with an operator or a lease to a tenant-operator. A recognised brand can help with distribution and financing, at the cost of fees and brand standards.

## Hotel due diligence checklist

Our standard checklist, adapted to each asset:

1. **Legal**: title, cadastre, easements, mortgages, litigation.
2. **Planning**: building permits, legalisations, compliance of floor areas and pools.
3. **Licences**: operating licence, classification, health and fire certificates.
4. **Financial**: three years of accounts, occupancy, ADR, forward bookings, contracts with platforms and tour operators.
5. **Technical**: building condition, plant, energy, costed refurbishment needs.
6. **Employment**: staff contracts, seasonal workforce, applicable agreements.
7. **Tax**: taxes and social contributions up to date, particularly in a share deal.
8. **Environmental**: protected areas, coastline, water supply.

## Financing and incentives

Greek banks lend to the hotel sector, usually with significant equity and on the basis of a trading history. Development Law 4887/2022 includes an aid scheme for tourism investments (creating, expanding or upgrading accommodation), which can take the form of tax exemptions, grants, leasing subsidies or support for employment costs. Access depends on open calls, the project and its region, and should be prepared early with a specialist adviser.
